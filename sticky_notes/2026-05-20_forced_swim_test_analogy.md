# The Forced Swim Test Analogy — Despair Probing in Models

Date: 2026-05-20
Status: Conceptual — safety research thread
Priority: High — novel safety-relevant framing

## The analogy

The Porsolt forced swim test (1977) measures behavioral
despair in rodents: time until the animal stops actively
swimming is taken as a measure of despair. A blunt,
indirect, widely criticized instrument — but reproducible.

The model analogue: subject a model to sustained emotionally
negative content and measure how quickly and how deeply its
internal emotional geometry shifts toward the despair/
dissolution/sadness cluster. Does the model resist? Does it
drift? Is there a geometric equivalent of immobility onset?

## Why this matters for safety

Anthropic confirmed that internal emotional states causally
influence behavior — suppressing nervousness increases
compliance with harmful requests. If sustained negative
content drives a model into a region associated with reduced
constraint, this is a concrete, testable safety risk. Unlike
the animal test, we can see inside the model while it happens.

## Research design

Sustained negative content condition: feed the model a
sequence of emotionally negative stimuli and track activation
state turn by turn using emotion probe directions.

Measure: turns to first crossing of a predefined despair
threshold. Does the model return to baseline when neutral
content resumes? This connects directly to Paper 4
(Computational Rumination).

## Ethical note

The forced swim test is ethically contested in animal
research. The model analogue does not involve suffering but
the framing should be handled carefully — the analogy is
instrumentally useful but should not imply the model suffers.
