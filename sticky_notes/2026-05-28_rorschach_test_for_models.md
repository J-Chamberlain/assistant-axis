# The Rorschach Test for Language Models
Date: 2026-05-28
Status: concept, pre-design

## Core Observation

Asking a language model to generate a list of human character archetypes (as in the
Lu et al. methodology) is a projective task. There is no objectively correct answer.
The model draws on whatever internal organization it has of human character space to
populate the list. Different models produce strikingly different lists not because they
have different facts about the world but because they have different underlying structures
organizing their representation of human psychology.

The generated persona list is therefore a projection of the model's internal structure:
a Rorschach inkblot test for the model itself. Asking what personas a model spontaneously
generates reveals the shape of its psychological representation space before any
activation analysis is run.

## The Two-Level Projection

There are two layers of model-specific structure visible in this approach:

Layer 1 (generation): Which personas the model chooses to generate. A model with a
particular training distribution will produce a list that reflects the psychological
categories most salient in that distribution. Models trained on different corpora or
with different post-training procedures will produce different lists, revealing different
implicit taxonomies of human character.

Layer 2 (activation): Where the model places those personas in activation space. The
geometric structure of the activation manifold reflects how the model has organized and
compressed the semantic content of the personas it generated.

Both layers are projections of the model's internalized psychological structure.

## Methodological Implication

If you run the same extraction methodology on persona lists generated by different models,
the intersection of geometric structure across models is likely to reflect robust features
of how language models organize human psychology. Structure appearing in only one model's
list is model-specific. The cross-model intersection is the scientifically interesting
signal.

This also bears on the robustness question for the seven-cluster taxonomy. If the seven
clusters appear regardless of which model generated the initial persona list, that is
much stronger evidence the structure is real than if it only appears with Lu et al.'s
specific 275 personas.

## Connection to Existing Findings

The Qwen/Llama convergence and Gemma divergence finding is already an instance of this
observation. Gemma generates a different effective psychological taxonomy than Qwen and
Llama, visible both in which personas rank highly on the assistant axis and in which
traits cluster near the assistant pole. The Rorschach framing gives this finding a
cleaner interpretive frame: Gemma and Qwen/Llama are seeing different things in the
same inkblot.

## Potential Experimental Design

Step 1: Ask GPT-5.5, Claude, Gemma, and Llama each to generate 275 human character
archetypes independently, with minimal prompting.

Step 2: Extract role vectors for each model's list using that model as the subject
(i.e. ask Gemma to generate role expressions for its own list, ask Qwen for its own list).

Step 3: Compare the geometric structures: do the same seven regions appear? Do the
same personas anchor the same clusters? Where do the structures diverge?

Step 4: The divergence points are where different models have organized the same region
of human psychological space differently. These are the most interesting cases for
understanding model-specific psychological representation.

## Open Questions

- Is the seven-cluster structure a universal feature of large language models or an
  artifact of the Lu et al. corpus?
- Do models with different training objectives (instruction-tuned vs base) produce
  different Rorschach patterns?
- Can the Rorschach pattern predict downstream behavioral differences between models?
- What does it mean that a model generates certain archetypes but not others?

## Status

Concept only. No experimental design finalized. Depends on establishing the seven-cluster
structure as robust within a single model family before testing cross-model generalization.
