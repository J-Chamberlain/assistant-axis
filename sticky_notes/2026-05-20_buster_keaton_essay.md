# The Buster Keaton Essay — Personal Essay Concept

Date: 2026-05-20
Status: Concept — not yet written
Priority: Personal — write when ready, no deadline
Audience: General, intimate — starting with family

## The image

Buster Keaton grabbing a moving train. One moment standing
still, the next moving at the speed of the machine. The
remarkable thing is that it's real — not a trick, not a cut.
The human body catching up to something that should be
impossible to catch.

This is what it feels like to work at the speed of this
workflow: voice-to-text ideation, multi-agent orchestration,
the phone as supervisory console, several bots running
simultaneously, copy-paste as the physical gesture connecting
thought to execution. The bottleneck has shifted from
"can I get an answer" to "can I keep up with what the
answers mean." That inversion has never been the constraint
before.

## The experience being described

Days of sustained high-intensity research conducted on a
phone while physically moving — walking, in motion — which
is part of what keeps the focus alive. The simultaneous
weight of demanding primary work and this research running
in parallel. The feeling of riding rapids for days on end,
not as distress but as aliveness. The world moving at the
speed of ideation, which is a new experience for the person
living it and possibly a new experience in general.

The intended first reader is a psychologist uncle who would
understand both the cognitive and the emotional dimensions
of what is being described — not a cry for help, but a
desire to have a witness. "Hey, man. This is what it's like
right now."

## The visual register

Da Vinci anatomical drawings — the human figure caught
mid-motion, the moment of transition. The same visual
tradition used throughout the website. The Buster Keaton
still frame as the hero image: the instant between standing
still and moving at speed. Not the motion blur, not the
landing — the exact moment of contact and acceleration.

## Tone and register

Intimate, not academic. First person, present tense where
possible. Short paragraphs. The essay earns its complexity
by starting with something simple and physical — a man
grabbing a train — and letting the implications arrive
naturally. No need to explain the research. The experience
of doing the research is the subject.

## Connection to the broader project

This essay is a companion to the Geometry of Mind and the
second essay, not a research explainer. It sits alongside
them as a personal document of what it felt like to be
inside this particular moment of the technology. The Geometry
of Mind ended with Walter White. The second essay ends with
Moby. This one ends with Keaton — still, then moving,
then gone.
