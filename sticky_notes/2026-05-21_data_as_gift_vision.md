# Data as Gift — Public Communication Vision

Date: 2026-05-21
Status: Ongoing vision — predates this research project
Priority: Personal/public — no deadline, high importance

## The core gift

A unique ability to make data come to life — to find the
insight inside a dataset that others cannot see and render
it in a form that is both precise and felt. This has been
recognized within every institution worked in. The goal
is to give it to the world rather than to any single
institution's agenda.

## The public communication mission

The AI field currently lacks tenderness. There is a growing
movement of fear and anger toward AI systems and the people
building them. Some of this is understandable — fear is
what people do when they don't understand something large
and fast-moving. But the unidirectionality of the anger
is misguided, and meeting it with more technical argument
is not the answer. What is missing is intimacy — work that
helps people feel something true about what these systems
are, not just understand it abstractly.

This research has the potential to do that. The emotion
field visualization, the floating words, the Moby
connection — these are not just research outputs. They
are invitations to feel something about AI that the
current discourse does not make room for.

## Museum-scale exhibits

The vision of human-scale, physically immersive data
exhibits has been present for years. AI now makes this
possible in ways it was not before. A room-scale
installation showing the emotional geometry of language
models in real time, or a visualization of how a
conversation drifts through persona space, could reach
people who would never read a research paper. This is
worth pursuing as a parallel track alongside the academic
work.

## The Ghostbusters connection

Ghostbusters II: the slime was reactive to negative
emotion — anger specifically caused it to bubble and
release destructive energy. The analogy to AI systems
whose internal emotional geometry is driven by the
emotional content of their inputs is striking and
accessible. Could be a lead-in for a public essay or
exhibit framing: what happens when you sing to the slime
versus when you yell at it? The research has an answer.
