# Autonomy, Collaboration, and Career Architecture

Date: 2026-05-21
Status: Ongoing personal reflection
Priority: Personal — no deadline

## The core tension

Independent research without institutional overhead feels
right. No meetings, no permission, no agenda except
curiosity. The work happens on its own terms as long as
the time can be made, and the time is being made. The
question is whether any institutional arrangement can
preserve that feeling rather than eroding it.

## The fellowship framing

The Anthropic Fellows Program is a two-way door — brief,
bounded, reversible. It would not be insane to take it.
It provides the two things independent research currently
lacks: access to frontier models and proximity to the
people doing the most relevant adjacent work. The autonomy
cost is temporary and scoped.

## The longer-term architecture

What feels right beyond the fellowship: being paid to do
this research on one's own terms, while remaining in
genuine collaboration with Anthropic specifically. Not an
employee relationship. Something more like a commissioned
researcher or long-term research partner. The one-way
door test (Bezos): no full-time institutional commitment
until there is very high confidence it preserves rather
than constrains the work. The fellowship itself is not a
one-way door. A full-time role might be.

## What Anthropic provides that is hard to replicate
- Frontier model access (Claude internals, emotion vectors,
  causal intervention capability)
- A community of people thinking about the same problems
- Credibility and distribution for the research outputs
- Budget for compute that currently limits the research

## What independent work provides
- Full agenda control
- Ability to follow unexpected threads immediately
- No obligation to frame findings in institutionally
  convenient ways
- The particular creative energy of working without
  an audience until the work is ready
