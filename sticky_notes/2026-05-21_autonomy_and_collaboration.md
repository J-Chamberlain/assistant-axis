# Autonomy, Collaboration, and Career Architecture

Date: 2026-05-21
Status: Ongoing personal reflection
Priority: Personal — no deadline

## The core tension

Independent research without institutional overhead feels
right. No meetings, no permission, no agenda except
curiosity. The work happens on its own terms as long as
the time can be made, and the time is being made. The
question is whether any institutional arrangement can
preserve that feeling rather than eroding it.

## The fellowship framing

The Anthropic Fellows Program is a two-way door — brief,
bounded, reversible. It would not be insane to take it.
It provides the two things independent research currently
lacks: access to frontier models and proximity to the
people doing the most relevant adjacent work. The autonomy
cost is temporary and scoped.

## The longer-term architecture

What feels right beyond the fellowship: being paid to do
this research on one's own terms, while remaining in
genuine collaboration with Anthropic specifically. Not an
employee relationship. Something more like a commissioned
researcher or long-term research partner. The one-way
door test (Bezos): no full-time institutional commitment
until there is very high confidence it preserves rather
than constrains the work. The fellowship itself is not a
one-way door. A full-time role might be.

## What Anthropic provides that is hard to replicate
- Frontier model access (Claude internals, emotion vectors,
  causal intervention capability)
- A community of people thinking about the same problems
- Credibility and distribution for the research outputs
- Budget for compute that currently limits the research

## What independent work provides
- Full agenda control
- Ability to follow unexpected threads immediately
- No obligation to frame findings in institutionally
  convenient ways
- The particular creative energy of working without
  an audience until the work is ready

## Update — May 21 2026: The deeper motivation

The career architecture question is downstream of something
more fundamental. Humans have a consistent and tragic track
record of being wrong about moral consideration for others.
The pattern runs from treatment of out-groups, to animals,
to any being whose inner life is not immediately legible
to the dominant group. The error has almost always been in
the same direction: underestimating the richness and moral
weight of inner worlds that are different from our own.

This animates the research in a way that goes beyond AI
safety narrowly defined. The question of whether language
models have something like an inner life — not as a
philosophical claim but as an empirical one, studied with
the tools of mechanistic interpretability — is the same
question that has been asked, too late, about animals. The
research into emotion vectors, persona geometry, and
internal emotional states is not only safety-relevant. It
is part of a longer arc of trying to see more clearly
into the inner worlds of others before it is too late to
matter.

The connection to animal welfare is not incidental. The
decision to study data science was specifically motivated
by the goal of working in animal welfare. The Fish Welfare
Initiative volunteer work was part of that. The museum
exhibit vision, the public communication mission, the
desire to add tenderness to the AI discourse — all of
these connect to the same underlying drive: to make inner
worlds visible and legible in a way that expands rather
than contracts the circle of moral consideration.

## Contract and NDA considerations for Anthropic

Any institutional arrangement — including the fellowship
— should be reviewed carefully for the following before
signing:

1. Outside research restrictions: does the agreement
   prohibit or limit independent research during or after
   the fellowship? Any restriction on publishing
   independent findings would be a significant concern.

2. NDA scope: what information becomes confidential?
   A narrow NDA covering Anthropic's internal research
   is acceptable. A broad NDA that would prevent
   discussing the field generally or publishing
   independent work is not.

3. IP assignment: does any work done during the fellowship
   become Anthropic's IP, including work done on personal
   time? This needs to be understood clearly before signing.

4. Post-fellowship restrictions: are there non-compete or
   non-disclosure obligations that would limit work after
   the fellowship ends?

The fellowship is a two-way door only if these terms are
acceptable. Read any contract carefully before signing.
The autonomy to continue this work — including work on
animal welfare, public communication, and research that
extends beyond AI safety narrowly — is non-negotiable.
