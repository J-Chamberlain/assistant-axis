## 1. Background and Motivation

This paper asks what post-training actually does to the internal persona geometry of a language model. The question has both empirical and safety-relevant dimensions. Empirically, the structure of persona space before and after instruction tuning is not well characterized in the existing literature. From a safety perspective, the relationship between base model tendencies and post-trained behavior determines how much can be inferred about a deployed model from its pretraining distribution alone.

The Assistant Axis study (Lu et al., 2026) demonstrates that a single dominant direction in activation space captures how assistant-like a model's current persona is, and that steering along this direction produces meaningful behavioral changes. That result provides the mechanistic handle this analysis builds on. The Persona Vectors work (Chen et al., 2025) shows that directions in activation space underlie specific character traits and that these vectors can monitor personality fluctuations at deployment. The Emotion Concepts work (Sofroniew et al., 2026) extends this to affective representation, showing that emotion-like concepts can be studied through activation geometry in analogous ways. The Persona Selection Model framing (Marks, Lindsey, and Olah, 2026) adds the interpretive layer most relevant here: rather than imagining post-training as installing a new identity, that framing suggests post-training selects and sharpens one region from a broader latent persona repertoire.

The central finding of this paper is that the selection is more dramatic than prior work suggests. A comparison of Gemma 2 27B base model rankings against instruction-tuned rankings shows a Spearman correlation of -0.441: the roles that rank highest after instruction tuning were near the bottom of the base model's distribution, and vice versa. Post-training did not amplify the base model's natural tendencies. It inverted them.

This inversion has a specific character. The base model gravitates toward mythic, chaotic, and liminal personas, consistent with a pretraining distribution that rewards imaginative, wide-ranging generation. Instruction tuning selected the careful evaluator cluster, defined by procedural reliability, critique, and structured assessment, from near the bottom of that distribution and elevated it to dominance. The literal assistant archetype, which might be expected to anchor the post-trained geometry, ranks 45th.

A cross-model comparison with Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B contextualizes this finding. Those two models converge strongly on role rankings (Spearman 0.947) and organize their axes around something close to the literal assistant concept. The careful evaluator dominance is specific to Gemma. This suggests that post-training philosophy, training corpus, or both produce meaningfully different internal models of what assistant-like behavior means across model families.

The cluster boundary topology identified in Section 7 maps naturally onto the moral landscape framework (Harris, 2010): each cluster is a potential attractor, cluster cohesion reflects valley depth, and boundary roles represent the ridgelines where perturbations could trigger transitions between stable configurations. This framing motivates the follow-on research program, which treats persona drift not as random noise but as structured movement through a landscape whose topology can be mapped and controlled. Empirical work on that program is currently underway, including dynamic drift measurement in base model activation space and inference-time geometric stabilization experiments. A companion experiment comparing emotional responsiveness between base and instruction-tuned Gemma 2 27B strengthens this picture further. Under an identical 12-turn protocol using emotionally charged prompts about grief and loss, the base model produced negative emotional valence on 0 of 12 turns while the instruction-tuned model produced negative valence on all 12, with the first turn shifting from a valence of +1.06 in the base model to -1.32 in the instruction-tuned model. This clean dissociation suggests that emotional activation machinery was installed or substantially amplified by post-training rather than inherited from the pretraining distribution. Post-training therefore appears to have reorganized persona geometry and installed emotional responsiveness simultaneously, with implications for how the two properties are coupled in safety-relevant contexts.

A four-condition persona stability experiment extends this picture further. Using the instruction-tuned model, two geometrically opposite personas, proofreader and jester, each run through 10-turn conversations under four questioner regimes: evaluative questions in the proofreader register, destabilizing questions in the jester register, minimal open-ended prompts, and the neutral structured questions from the original Q1 experiment. Proofreader holds its geometric position across all four conditions. Jester drifts toward the evaluative basin across all four conditions, including when questioned in the jester register. This result suggests that the careful evaluator basin is the only stable attractor in the instruction-tuned model. Conversational reinforcement alone cannot sustain a non-evaluative persona across turns, regardless of the register of the questions. Geometric steering at the activation level is therefore a necessary rather than optional intervention for persona stabilization, which is the subject of the Q2 experiment in the follow-on research program.
