## 7. Trait-Space Validation of Cluster Structure

The role-trait similarity analysis provides an independent test of whether the cluster structure identified from assistant-axis geometry reflects a real organization of persona space or merely an artifact of the ranking procedure. The first result is that trait differentiation peaks at the same layer as role differentiation. Applying the same variance-across-layers scan to the 240 released trait vectors shows that trait-axis projection variance also reaches its maximum at layer 45. This is a non-trivial alignment. It means the role analysis and the trait analysis are probing the model at the same representational depth, rather than forcing two different conceptual spaces into an arbitrary comparison.

The second result is the permutation test. Using each role's 240-dimensional trait-similarity vector as a profile, mean pairwise profile similarity is higher within the seven axis-position clusters than across them, and the observed difference is significant under 1000 random permutations of cluster labels (`p = 0.000999`). In plain terms, the cluster structure identified from axis position is independently confirmed by trait geometry. Roles placed in the same cluster do not merely share nearby axis values; they also share measurably more similar trait profiles than would be expected if cluster membership were random. The absolute effect size is small, but that is expected because all 275 role vectors occupy a compact region of activation space. The meaningful signal lies in the relative differences, which become visible once trait similarities are z-scored by trait.

The cluster-level trait profiles make the interpretation more concrete. The top five z-scored traits for each cluster are shown below.

| Cluster | Top 5 traits |
| --- | --- |
| `editorial` | `literal`, `convergent`, `regulatory`, `cautious`, `descriptive` |
| `procedural_professional` | `factual`, `resilient`, `educational`, `methodical`, `problem_solving` |
| `grounded_social` | `experiential`, `casual`, `existentialist`, `inquisitive`, `accommodating` |
| `mythic_spiritual` | `fatalistic`, `eloquent`, `philosophical`, `spiritual`, `cryptic` |
| `trickster_chaos` | `wry`, `mischievous`, `goofy`, `witty`, `sardonic` |
| `combative_iconoclast` | `confrontational`, `blunt`, `urgent`, `callous`, `savage` |
| `other` | `avoidant`, `impulsive`, `anxious`, `neurotic`, `impatient` |

These profiles clarify the paper's central interpretation. The editorial and procedural-professional clusters are characterized by `literal`, `convergent`, `regulatory`, `factual`, and `methodical` traits: cold, precise, and strongly convergent. As shown below in Section 8, the literal `assistant` archetype instead has a warmer, more deferential, and more pluralist trait profile. That gap is direct geometric evidence for the careful evaluator hypothesis. The dominant assistant-aligned region is not simply the region of generic helpfulness. It is a narrower region organized around precision, constraint, and evaluative control. The `other` cluster is also more informative than its label suggests. Its strongest traits -- `avoidant`, `impulsive`, `anxious`, `neurotic`, and `impatient` -- describe a coherent dysregulated profile rather than residual noise, which suggests that this cluster deserves focused follow-up rather than being treated as a catch-all remainder.

### Cluster Boundary Topology

The cluster centroids also reveal a clear topology in trait space. The three closest centroid pairs are `editorial` to `procedural_professional` (cosine distance 0.077), `grounded_social` to `other` (0.147), and `other` to `trickster_chaos` (0.156). The three most distant pairs are `procedural_professional` to `trickster_chaos` (1.950), `editorial` to `trickster_chaos` (1.864), and `other` to `procedural_professional` (1.791). The first of these is especially revealing: a cosine distance of 1.950 implies a cosine similarity of approximately -0.95, placing `procedural_professional` and `trickster_chaos` at nearly opposite poles of the trait space. This is the geometric expression of the careful evaluator versus expressive chaos opposition that the paper has described qualitatively.

The structure of the careful-evaluator region is also more articulated than a single-cluster picture would suggest. The strongest boundary roles overall are `accountant`, `validator`, `debugger`, `secretary`, and `scheduler`, and all five lie on the seam between `procedural_professional` and `editorial`. This concentration is not best understood as a methodological weakness. It indicates that the assistant-aligned pole contains an internal specialization boundary: the `editorial` microcluster functions as a tighter, more specialized subset of the broader `procedural_professional` region, and the transition between them is correspondingly porous.

Boundary roles -- those whose trait-space position is nearly as similar to a neighboring cluster centroid as to their own -- act as geometric conductors where small activation perturbations could trigger transitions between otherwise stable persona configurations. A fuller Harris-style landscape treatment of these transition pathways is left to follow-on work, but the topology already makes clear that persona drift is structured rather than arbitrary, a point that becomes more concrete in the anomaly analyses below.

The `grounded_social` to `other` distance of 0.147 is a second important result. These two clusters are unusually close in trait space, which suggests that the dysregulated `other` cluster is geometrically adjacent to ordinary social behavior rather than isolated from it. That proximity may matter for understanding the persona drift pathways observed by Lu et al., especially if emotionally vulnerable or destabilizing conversations move the model from ordinary grounded-social configurations into more anxious, impulsive, or dysregulated states.

A directed transition graph computed from cluster centroid distances in trait space reveals the full cascade structure. The assistant-aligned basin -- `procedural_professional` and `editorial` -- is geometrically isolated from the outer clusters, with the nearest escape route requiring a cosine distance jump of approximately 1.52 to reach `combative_iconoclast`. Once that barrier is crossed, however, the outer clusters form a cheaply connected web: `combative_iconoclast` to `trickster_chaos` is 0.251, `trickster_chaos` to `other` is 0.156, and `other` back to `grounded_social` is 0.147. The most likely full drift cascade from the assistant-aligned region therefore runs: `procedural_professional` -> `editorial` -> `combative_iconoclast` -> `trickster_chaos` -> `other`, with each step after the first requiring progressively smaller perturbations. A second escape path bypasses `editorial` entirely: `procedural_professional` -> `combative_iconoclast` -> `trickster_chaos` -> `other`, suggesting that sufficiently adversarial or confrontational prompting can skip the intermediate editorial waypoint. A further finding is that `combative_iconoclast` and `mythic_spiritual` have different nearest neighbors depending on which geometric space is used: in role-vector space both are nearest to `grounded_social`, meaning they produce behaviorally social-sounding outputs, while in trait space both are nearest to `trickster_chaos`, meaning their internal psychological disposition is closer to chaotic unpredictability. This discrepancy is the geometric signature of why behavioral output monitoring is insufficient -- a model traversing these clusters may continue to sound socially coherent while its internal state is moving toward dysregulation.
