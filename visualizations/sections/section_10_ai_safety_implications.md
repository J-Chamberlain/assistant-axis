## 9. Implications for AI Safety and Psychology Research

For AI safety, the central implication is that interventions calibrated to the surface concept "assistant" may be misaligned with the internal geometry that post-training actually created. If steering, monitoring, or activation capping is meant to keep a model in its safest default persona region, the most natural candidate internal target, based on the current geometry, is not the linguistically generic assistant archetype but the procedural-professional and editorial region around it. The difference matters because the strongest exemplars of that region are not social helpers but checkers, reviewers, validators, and formal analysts. The cascade analysis further suggests that the assistant-aligned region is not fragile but isolated, requiring a large perturbation to escape, while the outer clusters form a cheaply connected web where continued drift is self-reinforcing once the initial barrier is crossed.

The cross-model trait comparison raises a distinct safety consideration. The geometry of what counts as assistant-aligned differs across model families in ways that have direct trait-level implications. A model whose internal assistant pole is partially organized around accessible, practical, and benevolent traits has a different behavioral profile than one whose assistant pole is partially organized around elitist, dogmatic, and psychopathy-adjacent traits. These differences are not visible from behavioral evaluation alone. The models may produce superficially similar outputs while encoding meaningfully different internal orientations. This is precisely the class of alignment failure that mechanistic interpretability is positioned to detect: subtle geometric differences in what the model considers normal assistant behavior, which would only manifest as behavioral divergence under specific perturbations or extended interaction. The trait divergence finding motivates including cross-model geometric comparison as a standard component of pre-deployment safety evaluation.

A further finding concerns the coupling between persona geometry and emotional responsiveness. A direct comparison of base model and instruction-tuned Gemma 2 27B under emotionally charged prompts finds that the base model produces no negative emotional valence across 12 turns of grief and loss prompts, while the instruction-tuned model produces negative valence on every turn. The valence shift on the first turn is from +1.06 to -1.32, a complete sign reversal. This suggests that the emotional activation machinery documented in the Emotion Concepts work (Sofroniew et al., 2026) is not a pretraining property but a post-training artifact. The practical implication is that the base model and the instruction-tuned model differ not only in persona geometry but in their capacity for emotional reactivity. Safety evaluations that test only instruction-tuned models are therefore testing a model that has both been geometrically reorganized and made emotionally reactive relative to its base distribution, and these two properties may not be independently adjustable.

The four-condition stability experiment adds a further safety-relevant finding. If the careful evaluator basin is the only stable attractor in the instruction-tuned model, then conversational drift toward that basin is not a failure mode but the default dynamic. A model being pushed toward harmful or chaotic outputs through adversarial prompting is working against a strong natural restoring force. This is reassuring in one sense: the model has a built-in tendency to return to the careful evaluator region. It is also a limitation: the same force that resists adversarial drift also resists legitimate persona assignments that require sustained non-evaluative character. Any application requiring a model to maintain a stable creative, expressive, or role-playing persona across extended interaction needs active geometric stabilization, not just prompt engineering.

For interpretability, the present results support the idea that high-level behavioral regularities can sometimes be read directly from activation geometry. The rankings, clusters, and anomaly neighborhoods do not merely describe surface style; they expose a structured internal manifold that can be inspected, compared across layers, and potentially targeted by steering interventions. Mechanistic interpretability and higher-level behavioral analysis therefore look less like competing descriptions and more like complementary approaches to the same structure.

For human psychology, the strongest open question comes from the poet result. If a model trained on broad human text and then post-trained for assistant behavior internalizes a geometry where evaluative precision and expressive creativity sit at opposite poles, that may reflect one of two things. It may reflect something real about the tension between procedural reliability and open-ended self-expression in human social cognition. Or it may reflect a narrower artifact of post-training objectives. The important point is that the question is empirically tractable. Comparing the same analysis across model families and training regimes would directly test it.
