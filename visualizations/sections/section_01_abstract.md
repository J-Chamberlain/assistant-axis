## Abstract

Using pre-computed persona vectors released with the Assistant Axis work and a series of follow-on experiments, this paper characterizes the internal persona geometry of Gemma 2 27B across 275 character archetypes and examines how that geometry was shaped by post-training. Three central findings are reported.

First, the dominant assistant-aligned direction in Gemma 2 27B is not best understood as an assistant axis in the ordinary semantic sense. The highest-ranked roles are dominated by proofreader-, screener-, validator-, and reviewer-like identities, suggesting that post-training selected for procedural reliability and structured assessment rather than generic helpfulness. The literal assistant archetype ranks 45th.

Second, a comparison of base model and instruction-tuned rankings reveals a Spearman correlation of -0.441, indicating that RLHF performed a near-complete inversion of the base model's persona geometry. The base model naturally prefers mythic, chaotic, and liminal personas (eldritch, wraith, jester, absurdist). Post-training elevated the careful evaluator cluster from near the bottom of that distribution to the dominant pole. This is the first empirical demonstration in this analysis that RLHF can perform wholesale geometric reorganization of persona space rather than merely refining existing tendencies.

Third, a three-model cross-model comparison (Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B, Llama 3.3 70B) shows that the careful evaluator finding is specific to Gemma. Qwen and Llama converge at Spearman 0.947 and organize their axes around something much closer to the literal assistant concept. The traits most convergently assistant-aligned across all three models are transparent, dispassionate, detached, and calm. Gemma-specific assistant-aligned traits include elitist, arrogant, dogmatic, and nihilistic, while Qwen and Llama share accessible, practical, and benevolent. These findings have direct implications for AI safety evaluation, steering calibration, and pre-deployment model assessment.
