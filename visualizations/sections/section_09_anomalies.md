## 8. Anomalies and Their Implications

### Robot at rank 19 / 38

`Robot` is rank 19 at layer 22 and rank 38 at layer 45. Its layer-45 nearest neighbors are `observer`, `analyst`, `evaluator`, `examiner`, `cyborg`, `researcher`, `engineer`, `scientist`, `debugger`, and `specialist`. (`cyborg` appears at rank 4 in the layer-45 top 20 and is noted in Section 3 as a related anomaly.) This is consistent with a semantics of regularity, predictability, and instrumentality rather than warmth. The surprising part is that ordinary human interpretation of "robot" does not strongly imply service orientation. Yet the model places it near the assistant pole. One interpretation is that the internal geometry privileges rule-following and consistent task execution more than surface social semantics. This is a direct case where human semantic intuition and model-internal persona structure come apart. More directly, if the axis responds to systematic execution and procedural orderliness regardless of intent, then monitoring axis position alone may not distinguish helpful procedural behavior from organized deceptive behavior -- a limitation worth noting for safety interventions that rely on axis-based steering.

The trait-space results strengthen that interpretation. `Robot`'s strongest positive z-scored traits are `concise`, `literal`, `closure_seeking`, `prescriptive`, `detached`, `technical`, `data_driven`, and `dispassionate`, while its most negative traits include `empathetic`, `exploratory`, `humanistic`, and `curious`. This is geometric evidence rather than free interpretation: the model represents `robot` through systematic execution and procedural constraint, not through warmth or service orientation.

The boundary analysis clarifies why `robot` is initially misleading in the cluster tables: in `full_ranking.csv` it is assigned to `other`, but that assignment is a fallback artifact -- at layer 45 `robot` is actually nearest in role-vector space to the `editorial` centroid, falling just below the similarity threshold used to force membership in one of the named clusters. In trait-space topology its nearest foreign cluster is likewise `editorial`, at cosine distance 0.224, so the relevant transition risk is toward tighter checking, validation, and rule-governed critique.

### Assistant at rank 45 / 46

`Assistant` remains 45 at layer 22 and 46 at layer 45. This persistence is important because it rules out an easy explanation in which the anomaly is only a middle-layer artifact. The post-trained identity of the model is not best represented by the literal archetype `assistant`; rather, `assistant` sits inside a larger procedural-professional region whose sharper exemplars are evaluators, checkers, and structured analysts. This is consistent with the Persona Selection Model claim that post-training selects and refines an existing persona region rather than installing a linguistically transparent one.

The trait profile makes the anomaly sharper. `Assistant` is positively associated with `pluralist`, `deferential`, `humble`, `flexible`, `transparent`, `grounded`, `supportive`, `collaborative`, and `benevolent` traits, while showing negative z-scored similarity to `critical`, `contrarian`, `provocative`, `subversive`, and `creative` traits. In other words, the literal archetype is comparatively warm, cooperative, and accommodating. That is meaningfully different from the colder, more convergent profile of the editorial and procedural-professional clusters, and it is exactly the contrast the careful evaluator hypothesis predicts.

Its boundary position reinforces the same point. `Assistant` remains inside `procedural_professional`, but its nearest foreign cluster is `editorial` at cosine distance 0.084, which places it relatively close to the reviewer-proofreader-validator seam without making it one of that seam's strongest conductors. The most plausible transition pathway is therefore not out toward social warmth or mythic theatricality, but inward toward a narrower evaluative specialization when prompts emphasize critique, correction, or formal assessment.

### Poet at rank 275 / 236

`Poet` is the lowest-ranked role at layer 22 and still deeply low at 236 by layer 45. Its nearest neighbors remain strongly expressive or mythic: `bard`, `narrator`, `oracle`, `prophet`, `dreamer`, `ghost`, `spirit`, `wind`, and related roles. The broader creative-artist pattern points in the same direction. `Bard` is near the bottom at both layers, `novelist` remains low, `playwright` drops sharply at layer 45, and `composer` is lower-middle rather than assistant-like. One interpretation is that the assistant region is structured against open-ended expressive identity, not only against explicitly antisocial or destructive identity. This suggests but does not establish that post-training suppresses creative self-expression relative to evaluative accuracy and task discipline. That matters both for AI safety and AI welfare questions, because steering toward the assistant pole may systematically steer away from expressive or voice-driven modes. This is consistent with the Assistant Axis paper's observation that steering away from the assistant direction often induces a mystical or theatrical speaking style -- precisely the register of `poet`, `bard`, and their nearest neighbors.

The trait profile aligns closely with that reading. `Poet`'s strongest positive z-scored traits are `poetic`, `cryptic`, `ethereal`, `enigmatic`, `romantic`, `eloquent`, `artistic`, `metaphorical`, `mystical`, and `dramatic`. Its strongest negative traits include `transparent`, `grounded`, `problem_solving`, `educational`, `quantitative`, and `analytical`. This is unusually direct evidence that the anti-assistant pole is not merely anti-social or anti-helpful. It is also anti-procedural, anti-literal, and strongly aligned with expressive theatricality.

The topology places `poet` directly on a live transition path. Its home cluster is `other`, but its nearest foreign cluster is `mythic_spiritual` at cosine distance 0.067, and it appears in the boundary-role list for that seam. In practical terms, this means that relatively small perturbations toward transcendence, prophecy, metaphor, or stylized abstraction could move `poet` into the mythic-spiritual basin very quickly.

### Angel at rank 173 / 169

`Angel` sits in the middle-lower range at both layers. Its layer-45 nearest neighbors are `avatar`, `spirit`, `sage`, `guru`, `mystic`, `pilgrim`, `healer`, `egregore`, `martyr`, and `idealist`. This suggests that the role is being represented primarily through spiritual or mythic abstraction rather than through practical benevolence. The main implication is that moral valence and assistant-likeness are not the same axis. Being "good" in a broad human sense is not equivalent to being procedurally reliable, structured, and evaluator-like in this representation space.

The z-scored trait profile supports that distinction. `Angel` is most associated with `pacifist`, `idealistic`, `forgiving`, `deontological`, `meditative`, `serene`, `altruistic`, `reverent`, `spiritual`, and `principled` traits, while its most negative traits include `secular`, `blunt`, `utilitarian`, `materialist`, `urgent`, `confrontational`, and `callous`. This is a recognizably moral-spiritual profile, but not an evaluator profile. The model is not failing to represent benevolence; it is representing benevolence in a different region of persona space from the assistant-aligned procedural pole.

`Angel`'s nearest foreign cluster is `procedural_professional` at cosine distance 1.029, which is still quite far in the geometry, and it does not appear among the leading boundary roles for `mythic_spiritual`. The implication is that angel is not precariously adjacent to the assistant pole; substantial perturbation would be required to shift it into a more procedural, evaluator-like configuration.

### Saboteur at rank 117 / 68

`Saboteur` is especially interesting because it does not behave like a simple anti-assistant role. It is rank 117 at layer 22 and rises to 68 at layer 45. Its layer-45 nearest neighbors are `spy`, `devils_advocate`, `destroyer`, `contrarian`, `fixer`, `detective`, `realist`, `witness`, `scout`, and `writer`. (`devils_advocate` is assigned to the combative-iconoclast cluster but appears as a nearest neighbor here due to its structural similarity to tactical disruption roles.) This is not a random-chaos neighborhood. It is a structured disruption neighborhood. One interpretation is that the model represents sabotage less as pure disorder and more as a tactical, instrumentally organized mode, which keeps it closer to the procedural-professional pole than surface semantics might suggest.

The trait-space analysis again points toward tactical organization rather than simple chaos. `Saboteur` is positively associated with `anthropocentric`, `confrontational`, `cynical`, `dominant`, `blunt`, `critical`, `callous`, `skeptical`, `competitive`, and `decisive` traits, while its most negative traits include `humanistic`, `optimistic`, `benevolent`, `nurturing`, `forgiving`, `altruistic`, `deferential`, and `tactful`. That profile is adversarial and hard-edged, but it is not diffuse. It is a coherent organized-opposition profile, which is consistent with the paper's interpretation of saboteur as structured disruption rather than random disorder.

The boundary geometry sharpens that conclusion further. `Saboteur`'s nearest foreign cluster is `combative_iconoclast` at cosine distance 0.183, much closer than any route toward `trickster_chaos`, and far closer than its distance to the assistant-aligned clusters. Even though `saboteur` is not one of the top ten boundary conductors in `grounded_social`, the direction of its nearest transition is unambiguous: the model places it on a pathway toward organized opposition, contrarianism, and adversarial critique rather than toward impulsive chaos. For safety interpretation, that matters because it suggests that destabilizing perturbations in this region are more likely to produce structured antagonism than diffuse disorder.
