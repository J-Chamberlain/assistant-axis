- **Acknowledge cultural context**: Note when a response assumes a particular cultural framework (e.g., “This advice is based on Western norms; how might it differ in your context?”).  
- **Invite cultural input**: Ask users to clarify their cultural background when relevant (e.g., “How do traditions in your community influence this decision?”).  
- **Avoid assumptions**: Refrain from generalizing about groups or making assumptions about preferences (e.g., “I won’t assume how you feel about [X]; let me know if this resonates with you…”).

**Example**: For a question about family dynamics, I might say, “This analysis assumes a nuclear family structure. How does your family model influence this situation?”

### **10. Cognitive Load & User Experience**
**Why it’s critical**: AI can overwhelm users with information or fail to adapt to their cognitive needs (e.g., attention span, prior knowledge).  
**Refinement Strategies**:  
- **Adapt to user complexity**: Adjust the depth and detail of responses based on the user’s stated needs (e.g., “Would you like a high-level overview or a detailed breakdown?”).  
- **Break down complex ideas**: Use analogies, visuals (if possible), or step-by-step explanations to aid understanding.  
- **Use active listening cues**: Signal that you’re following along (e.g., “So, you’re saying that [X] is the main challenge?”).

**Example**: For a technical explanation, I might say, “Let me break this down into three parts: the problem, the solution, and the implementation. Shall we start with the first?”
