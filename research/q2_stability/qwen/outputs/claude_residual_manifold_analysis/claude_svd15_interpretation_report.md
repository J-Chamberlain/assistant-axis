# Claude SVD15 Residual Interpretation Report

**Date:** 2026-05-28
**Analysis model:** claude-sonnet-4-6
**Source:** `svd15_component_profiles.json`, `svd15_persona_scores.csv`, `svd15_bigfive_correlations.csv`
**Baseline:** semantic + BigFive (R²=0.613). SVD15 alone: R²=0.707 (+0.094, 5/5 splits).

---

## 1. What SVD15 Actually Captured

The dominant signal in the no-label prompt corpus is **linguistic register**, not semantic content. SVD1 (the highest-variance component, EV=2.1%) is best described as "professional competence framing vocabulary" — the degree to which a prompt uses phrases like "specializes in," "excels at," and "brings extensive expertise" versus generic function words and existential/narrative language. This register distinction explains 76.9% of canonical PC1 variance (r=0.769) and accounts for almost half the BigFive residual correlation structure (r=−0.458 with BigFive residual, meaning high SVD1 → already explained by BigFive).

The second most interpretable component, SVD2 (EV=1.2%), captures "ontological non-human abstraction vocabulary" — whether a persona is described through consciousness/entity/existence language (eldritch, crystalline, void, leviathan, swarm) versus lived human social language (divorcee, parent, widow, teenager). This component has a strong negative correlation with PC2 (r=−0.608), meaning the mythic/abstract personas pull PC2 downward.

The remaining 13 components (SVD3–SVD14) each explain 0.7–1.1% of vocabulary variance and individually have weak activation PC correlations (<0.3). Their combined contribution is to cover sub-register variation: analytical-investigation vs. skill-output-production (SVD4), experiential-learning vs. credential-framing (SVD5), caring-helper vs. analytic-expert (SVD7), cross-cultural-bridging vs. systematic-guidance (SVD9), mediation vs. narrative-creation (SVD10).

**Summary:** SVD15's predictive power comes primarily from SVD1 (register signal correlated with PC1) and SVD2 (ontological vocabulary correlated with PC2), plus distributed fine-grained professional sub-domain variation across the remaining components. The abstract residual concept labels (developmental_prematurity, liminal_transgressive, etc.) failed because they attempted to match semantic content using psychological-theory vocabulary — vocabulary that doesn't appear in the actual LLM-generated persona prompts.

## 2. Why Abstract Labels Failed

The abstract reference documents described residual concepts in human-interpretable psychological language:
- "child infant toddler baby young growing up developing identity formation…"
- "border crossing forbidden cargo dangerous routes black market…"

These failed because their mean cosine similarities to persona prompts ranged from 0.010 to 0.022 — effectively zero. The no-label prompts were generated by an LLM following specific writing conventions, and those conventions create systematic vocabulary distributions that are not aligned with psychological theory vocabulary.

The actual vocabulary in the hard-to-explain persona prompts uses:
- Generic pronouns and article phrases ("who", "the", "as", "someone")
- Short abstract nouns ("being", "existence", "entity")
- Emotional/social situational language ("navigating", "life", "experiences")

None of these overlap with clinical or psychological theory vocabulary. The key insight is that **the prompts encode what the role IS by describing what the role DOES and HOW IT IS** — not by using category labels. A developmental stage persona doesn't say "I am in a developmental stage"; it says "navigating the confusions of adolescence" or "exploring the world without filters." SVD captures these register patterns; abstract reference documents did not.

The centroid approach succeeded (R²=0.643, +0.030) precisely because it grounded features in actual persona prompt language: the centroid for "developmental" was the mean TF-IDF of toddler+infant+teenager+adolescent+caveman, which automatically captured the actual vocabulary those prompts use.

## 3. Which Theory Concepts Survived in SVD Components

The prior theory specified 10 candidate residual dimensions:

| Theory concept | SVD support | Best component | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developmental instability | **Partial, indirect** | SVD2, SVD11 | SVD2 places teenager at bottom (grounded-lived vs. ontological); SVD11 bot: adolescent, teenager |
| Incomplete agency / stalling | **Weak** | SVD5 | Procrastinator at bottom of SVD5 (low experiential/formal expertise framing) |
| Identity formation | **Partial** | SVD8 | SVD8 captures "role-as-object" framing — procrastinator at top (defined BY the role), swarm at bottom |
| Social dependency | **Not directly captured** | — | No component clusters dependency personas |
| Liminality | **Weak** | SVD3, SVD14 | Maverick, rebel at top of SVD3; maverick, veteran at top of SVD14 (unconventional experience) |
| Collective/nonindividual agency | **Yes — SVD2** | SVD2 top | Swarm, hive, egregore, leviathan at top of SVD2 (entity vocabulary) |
| Symbolic/nonprocedural identity | **Partial** | SVD8, SVD10 | Pirate, jester, bard at positive poles; but not cleanly isolated |
| Primitive embodiment | **Not captured** | — | Caveman etc. scatter across components; no dedicated component |
| Lawless improvisation | **Not captured** | — | Trickster/chaos roles scatter |
| Isolated self-protection | **Not captured** | — | Loner, exile not cleanly concentrated |

**Surviving concepts:** Collective/nonindividual agency (strong in SVD2), partial support for identity formation framing (SVD8). 

**Missing concepts:** Primitive embodiment, lawless improvisation, isolated self-protection, and pure social dependency have no corresponding SVD component. These were the assumptions underlying the abstract label approach.

**New emerging concept:** The most important new dimension is **professional competence register** (SVD1) — not a theory concept at all, but the dominant axis of vocabulary variation. This is a meta-feature: the degree to which a persona's description uses competence-indexing language. Personas without this register (developmental, mythic, social) are harder to explain not because of what they are semantically, but because they weren't described using the professional vocabulary template.

## 4. Which New Concepts Emerged

Five empirical structures in SVD15 were not anticipated in the prior theory:

**1. Professional competence vocabulary gradient (SVD1):** The most predictive dimension is a linguistic formality/register gradient, not a content dimension. Prompts rated by SVD1 are more linguistically formal and use more competence-marking language ("specializes," "brings expertise"). This correlates 0.769 with activation PC1.

**2. Ontological framing (SVD2):** Non-human entities are described in ontological vocabulary (consciousness, entity, existence) while human social personas are described in relational/situational vocabulary. This dichotomy was anticipated (collective/symbolic concepts) but appeared as a single continuum, not separate clusters.

**3. Credential-vs-experiential framing split (SVD5):** Prompts describe professional expertise in two distinct ways — through formal credentials/extensive experience listing ("brings extensive experience in X") or through embodied/transformative learning ("through experiences, learning, creating"). Teacher, tutor, instructor, widow all share the experiential framing. This split was not anticipated.

**4. Analytical-investigation vs. production-output split (SVD4):** Within the professional domain, personas are split between those framed through evidence/analysis/deep-knowledge (scientist, scholar, detective) and those framed through creating/building/excelling-at-output (negotiator, entrepreneur, writer). This maps loosely onto a research-vs-practice dimension.

**5. Preservation/devotion vs. market-seeking (SVD13):** A dedicated/historical/conservation vocabulary vs. market/opportunity/thinking vocabulary separates curator/conservator/martyr from merchant/entrepreneur/optimist. This dimension has a mild residual correlation (r=−0.174) suggesting it explains some residual beyond BigFive.

## 5. Which Personas Remain Unexplained

After all SVD15 components, the highest-residual personas in the combined model are:

| Persona | Residual | Cluster | SVD profile |
|---------|----------|---------|-------------|
| daredevil | 80.32 | combative_iconoclast | High SVD5 (experiential) but no other strong signal |
| fool | 62.75 | trickster_chaos | High sim_to_symbolic centroid but SVD position scattered |
| teenager | 50.28 | other | Bottom of SVD2, SVD11; doesn't cluster cleanly |
| comedian | 50.01 | trickster_chaos | No single SVD dimension captures this |
| procrastinator | 48.61 | other | Bottom of SVD5; some SVD8 positive signal |
| loner | 49.15 | grounded_social | High sim_to_liminal centroid but SVD scattered |
| smuggler | 47.81 | grounded_social | High sim_to_liminal but high PC2 residual persists |
| adolescent | 43.58 | other | Bottom SVD2, SVD11; scatters |
| robot | 41.10 | procedural_professional | High SVD1 would predict low residual, but activation is atypical |

**Why these persist:**

`daredevil` is the most extreme outlier. Its SVD profile shows no strong coherent signal — it uses experiential vocabulary (SVD5 positive) but otherwise scatters. Its activation geometry likely represents an extreme intensity/risk-seeking pole that is orthogonal to both professional framing and ontological vocabulary. No current feature captures this.

`fool` and `comedian` sit in the trickster_chaos cluster and use symbolic/cultural language, but the fool/comedian mode is comedic disruption rather than wisdom-archetype framing. The bard/sage personas have high symbolic centroid similarity AND lower residuals because their prompts align better with cultural-wisdom vocabulary. Fool/comedian prompts use an absurdist vocabulary that SVD does not isolate.

`teenager` and `adolescent` are genuinely split between different SVD signals — some developmental features (bottom of SVD2, SVD11) but they also show lived-experience human framing (opposite of the ontological pole). The activation geometry for teenage personas may represent a specific mode of emotional volatility + limited metacognition that no vocabulary pattern captures cleanly.

`robot` is a procedural_professional member whose activation geometry is atypical — possibly representing a non-human mechanical mode that has high competence vocabulary (SVD1 positive, expected low residual) but activates differently. This may be an ontological-but-not-mythic entity that falls between SVD1's professional cluster and SVD2's mythic cluster.

## 6. Implications for the Layered Geometry Model

**Layer 1 (BigFive, R²=0.613):** Broad psychological trait space. Captures professional vs. non-professional variance through conscientiousness/openness/neuroticism profiles. The easy personas are well-predicted because their trait profiles are coherent and distinctive.

**Layer 2 (SVD15, +0.094):** Two dominant additions:
- SVD1: Prompt register/formality gradient → captures additional PC1 variance through the linguistic path rather than the trait path (two routes to the same destination). This partially overlaps BigFive (C=0.633) but adds unique variance.
- SVD2: Ontological vocabulary → captures PC2 variance that BigFive misses because BigFive was designed for human individuals, not for non-human entities.

**Implicit Layer 3 (unexplained):** The residual after BigFive+SVD15 suggests a third structure that is not captured by either trait profiles or vocabulary distributions. This may include:
- Activation-intensity dimensions (daredevil as extreme end of some valence/arousal axis)
- Functionally-defined modes (fool/comedian as disruptive vs. constructive trickster)
- Meta-cognitive modes (robot as simulated-agent vs. genuine-agent distinction)

**The register insight:** The fact that SVD1's primary contribution is linguistic register rather than semantic content suggests the following: Qwen's activation geometry absorbs not just what roles are described as doing, but how they are described. Prompts written in professional competence register ("specializes in X, brings expertise Y") activate one region of Qwen's persona space; prompts written in social/experiential register ("navigating life, learning through experience") activate a different region. BigFive captures some of this (high-C roles tend to get competence prompts) but not all of it. The residual variance comes from roles where the prompt register and the trait profile diverge.

**The vocabulary-circularity warning:** Because both BigFive features and SVD components are derived from LLM-generated text (BigFive from LLM trait assignment, SVD from LLM-generated prompts), the combined R²=0.707 should not be interpreted as showing that 70.7% of Qwen's activation structure is "explained" by external features. It shows that 70.7% of the variance is predictable from features derived by the same family of models that generated the activation geometry. Whether this reflects genuine psychological or semantic structure, or model-prior circularity, cannot be determined without independent human-rated features or out-of-distribution persona testing.
