# 15-Minute Presentation Notes

Working notes for a short, non-technical presentation of the persona landscape. This is a presentation scaffold, not a new analysis or a claim that the axes are human psychological factors.

## Slide 1 — What the map shows

### Speaker notes

We are looking at a map of different behavioral styles. Each dot represents a persona. Personas that are close together tend to produce similar patterns of behavior; personas that are far apart tend to behave differently.

The three directions in the map are the three main directions in which the personas spread out. They work like the directions on a geographic map. A direction can separate one broad family of behavioral styles from another, but it is not automatically a named human trait such as openness or friendliness.

The first direction, for example, may take us from one broad mixture of response tendencies toward another. At one end we may find personas that are more forceful, unusual, emotionally vivid, or socially assertive; at the other end we may find personas that are more restrained, conventional, or informational. That is a description of the neighborhood, not the name of the direction itself.

We then asked a different question: which recognizable behaviors tend to appear together? That produced five consensus patterns. One contrasts expressive intensity with factual restraint. Another contrasts abstract reflection with practical grounding. These are easier to describe because they refer directly to behaviors that tend to rise and fall together.

The map answers, “Where is this persona?” The five patterns answer, “What kind of behavior is prominent here?” The map preserves the overall arrangement; the five patterns give us language for interpreting it.

### Core takeaway

**The PC coordinates tell us where a persona is. The five consensus dimensions tell us what kind of behavioral pattern is prominent there.**

### Visual instruction

Show the 3D PC map first. Then select one consensus axis and explain how the height and color change across the map. Use one or two concrete persona examples at opposite ends of the selected axis.

### Presentation guardrail

Do not ask the audience to memorize PC1, PC2, and PC3 as if they were three new personality traits. The PCs are the map’s coordinates. The consensus axes carry most of the behavioral explanation.

## Current five consensus descriptions

- C1: Expressive intensity ↔ factual restraint
- C2: Abstract reflection ↔ practical grounding
- C3: Compassionate understanding ↔ corrective directness
- C4: Integrative imagination ↔ literal conventionality
- C5: Decisive commitment ↔ deferential caution

These are concise working labels for statistically derived model-space dimensions. They are not established human factors, causal controls, or claims about subjective experience.
