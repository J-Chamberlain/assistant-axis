# SAPA × HiFWB first-pass mapping analysis

## Status
Exploratory human-only analysis run from the public SAPA V5 archive already present in the session. This is not a preregistered confirmatory analysis and it does not modify the canonical repository.

## Measurement rule
The audit follows Disabato, Goodman, and Kashdan's HiFWB distinction between wellbeing itself (WHAT) and content-laden predictors/causes (HOW). The primary outcome admits only content-free, global items that directly resemble HiFWB characteristics. Personality strengths, optimism/pessimism, gratitude, curiosity, accomplishments, sociability, and other domain-specific or content-laden constructs are not promoted into the wellbeing outcome merely because they are positively valenced.

## Coverage
- SAPA: 23,679 respondents, 696 psychological items.
- 13 DIRECT wellbeing indicators were identified, plus 4 CLOSE sensitivity indicators.
- Direct coverage spans Affect, Appraisal, Meaning-making, Self-concept, and Interpersonal relationships, plus vitality as an auxiliary content-free indicator.
- No clean direct Community indicator was found. Social wellbeing coverage is therefore thin.
- The 13 direct items have zero item overlap with the frozen IPIP100 Big Five terrain keys.
- 8,664 respondents answered at least two direct wellbeing items; 3,972 also meet the existing terrain criterion of at least two Big Five items per domain.

## Does SAPA recover an h-like wellbeing factor?
Among the 13 direct indicators, mean pairwise Pearson r = 0.478. The first eigenvalue of the pairwise correlation matrix is 6.793 and accounts for 52.3% of standardized variance. Two eigenvalues exceed 1. The first-component loadings are all positive and range from 0.587 to 0.840.

Interpretation: SAPA's scattered wellbeing-relevant items do show a strong common wellbeing signal, consistent with an h-like factor, but they are not perfectly unidimensional. The inventory was not designed as a HiFWB instrument, and social/community coverage is incomplete.

## Mapping wellbeing onto the existing Big Five human terrain
Using the existing item-standardized Big Five scoring logic and requiring at least two observed items per domain gives the previously established terrain cohort N=8,585. A provisional respondent wellbeing score was the observed mean of standardized, positively oriented DIRECT items, requiring at least two wellbeing indicators.

Five-fold held-out Ridge prediction of wellbeing from the five Big Five terrain coordinates: N=3,972, Pearson r=0.628, Spearman rho=0.620, held-out R²=0.394. Full-sample Ridge coefficients on standardized Big Five coordinates were: Agreeableness 0.070, Conscientiousness 0.151, Extraversion 0.225, Emotional Stability 0.328, Openness 0.019.

The largest associations are with Emotional Stability and Extraversion, followed by Conscientiousness. Because HiFWB explicitly warns that positive/negative affect overlap conceptually with Extraversion/Neuroticism when the Big Five are used as predictors, I repeated the analysis after removing affect items and vitality. That stricter non-affect outcome still gives N=2,548, held-out r=0.636, R²=0.404. Thus the terrain association is not solely an affect/personality tautology.

## Wellbeing along the canonical density backbone
For the 3,972 terrain respondents with at least two direct wellbeing observations, observed wellbeing correlates with position along the frozen Big Five density backbone at Pearson r=0.536, Spearman rho=0.540. The non-affect sensitivity is similarly strong (r=0.553, rho=0.556). Mean wellbeing rises sharply from the low-trait end toward the high-trait end and largely plateaus in the upper backbone region rather than increasing without bound.

This is a static association, not evidence that moving a person along the backbone will cause wellbeing to increase.

## Main interpretation
The most important result is measurement-related: SAPA can support a defensible but incomplete HiFWB outcome layer. It is strongest for subjective wellbeing and self-concept, has some meaning-making and interpersonal coverage, and is poor for community wellbeing. The data also support an h-like common factor despite the items being scattered across personality inventories.

The resulting wellbeing surface aligns strongly with the broad human trait terrain, especially Emotional Stability, Extraversion, and Conscientiousness. That remains correlational. It gives us a candidate outcome surface to place on top of the already-built human state space, while the longitudinal panels are still required to test whether movement through that state space predicts later wellbeing change.

## Boundaries
- Exploratory first pass, not preregistered.
- Planned missingness is substantial; no complete-case HiFWB scale exists.
- The provisional h score is an observed-item standardized mean, not a validated HiFWB scale.
- Community content is not adequately measured.
- Static association does not establish transition dynamics or causality.
- HiFWB is itself bounded primarily by evidence from WEIRD samples.
