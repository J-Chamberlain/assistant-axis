## Four-profile anchor

- **Profile A — less reflective/idea-engaged and more deferential/quiet.** Relative elevations occur on difficulty imagining, rarely seeking deeper meaning, waiting for others to lead, being influenced by others, and having little to say. Relative depressions occur on noticing beauty, reflection, discussion, novel ideas, and handling complex problems.
- **Profile B — socially comfortable and broadly upbeat.** The strongest relative elevations concern ease with people, making friends, low depressed affect, optimism, life satisfaction, and energetic follow-through. Worthlessness, directionlessness, self-dislike, and a dark outlook are strongly lower than in other profiles.
- **Profile C — distressed, self-doubting, and socially uncomfortable.** Worthlessness, misery, inability to cope, dark expectations, low mood, self-dislike, desperation, and difficulty approaching others are strongly elevated. Social ease, friendship formation, vitality, optimism, and life satisfaction are strongly depressed.
- **Profile D — socially assertive, agentic, and task-forward.** Friendship initiation, conversation, social skill, leadership, managing multiple demands, and problem solving are elevated. Waiting for others, intimidation, difficulty coping, time wasting, and trouble initiating or finishing tasks are depressed.

These four broad configurations reproduce only moderately across the independent split (mean aligned profile distance 0.091721). Raw response-style summaries classify the four MAP labels substantially above chance (replication balanced accuracy 0.6936 versus 0.25), so their apparent behavioral organization is inseparable from a serious response-style warning. At the same time, 99.0% of between-profile item-pattern variance remains after removing general profile-level response elevation; this argues against reducing the result to a single high-versus-low response tendency.

## Ten-profile validation anchor

- **Profile A — emotionally restrained and socially reserved.** Low emotional reactivity, low talkativeness, interpersonal distance, and low stress/panic distinguish it.
- **Profile B — socially inhibited and background-oriented.** Quietness, difficulty approaching others, avoiding attention, and low party/social initiation dominate the contrasts.
- **Profile C — rule-following, cautious, and nondeceptive.** Following rules and authority, reluctance to lie or cheat, and rejection of recklessness, scams, danger, and rebellion distinguish it.
- **Profile D — comparatively less reflective/imaginative and less emotionally intense.** Rarely seeking deeper meaning, difficulty imagining, and reduced reflection, vivid imagination, and emotional intensity are the clearest contrasts. This summary is comparatively diffuse and should be treated cautiously.
- **Profile E — energetic, socially confident, productive, and positive.** Social ease and initiative, sustained energy, active spare time, and low misery, self-doubt, inactivity, and social difficulty dominate the profile.
- **Profile F — highly outgoing and party-oriented.** Party participation, talkativeness, initiating social energy, mixing with people, and many friends are elevated; quietness, avoidance, and social approach difficulty are depressed.
- **Profile G — distressed and low in self-regard.** Inability to cope, worthlessness, discouragement, self-dislike, low mood, worry, and doubt are elevated; self-satisfaction, confidence, leadership, energy, and life satisfaction are depressed.
- **Profile H — nervous/subdued and low in display-seeking.** Nervousness, mood fluctuation, feeling short-changed, low self-opinion, taking time, and preference for reading over meeting people rise, while showing off, attention seeking, party interaction, rule breaking, and task noncompletion fall.
- **Profile I — affectively labile, impulsive, and impression-conscious.** Mood swings, emotional stirring, impulsive action, rushing, and impression management rise; emotional control, composure, quietness, and conventional restraint fall.
- **Profile J — rule-breaking, risk/power-oriented, and confrontational.** Rebellion, rule breaking, danger seeking, power motivation, admiration of scams, and willingness to exploit distinguish it; rule following, conventionality, reluctance to manipulate, and opposition to lying or cheating are depressed.

This finer solution has low independent split-refit stability (mean aligned distance 0.153987), so the ten descriptions should not be treated as a replicated ten-group taxonomy. Raw response-style summaries predict the ten MAP labels above chance (replication balanced accuracy 0.4013 versus 0.10), triggering the frozen warning. Several groups visibly differ in middle versus extreme category use, especially Profiles B/D versus E/H. Nevertheless, 96.5% of between-profile item-pattern variance remains after general response-level removal, so response style is an important correlate rather than a complete explanation.
