# SAPA Static Human Terrain — Big Five Peak/Saddle/Corridor Follow-up

## Bottom line

The frozen peak/saddle analysis does **not** resolve multiple material local density peaks in the five-dimensional Big Five terrain. Under the primary 60-NN density / symmetric 30-NN graph, the component-tree procedure finds one global/root density maximum plus only two finite-persistence bumps. Their basin supports are 18 respondents (0.210%) and 1 respondent (0.012%), both far below the preregistered 0.5% material-support floor of 43 respondents.

Because fewer than two finite-persistence descriptive peaks survive the frozen support rule, the preregistered corridor analysis stops here: there is no defensible pair of material Big Five peaks between which to define or interpret a saddle corridor. This is stronger evidence for a single broad occupancy mound at Big Five resolution than the earlier connected-component result alone.

## Relation to the prior result

The prior analysis established that high-density occupancy remains one connected component from the top 10% through the top 90% of respondent density. That result did not exclude the possibility of multiple local hills joined by lower-density necks.

This follow-up specifically tested that possibility. It processes respondents from highest to lowest density and records when locally born high-density components merge. If substantial hills existed, they would appear as finite-persistence peaks with nontrivial basin support before merging into the dominant component.

They do not, under the frozen material-support rule.

## Primary result

Primary cohort N = 8,585.

Material-support floor = 0.5% = 43 respondents.

Global/root peak local-neighborhood profile, expressed in standardized Big Five coordinates:

- Agreeableness: local mean +0.343, median +0.346
- Conscientiousness: local mean -0.013, median +0.003
- Extraversion: local mean +0.116, median +0.092
- Emotional Stability: local mean +0.574, median +0.557
- Openness: local mean +0.447, median +0.456

The root density maximum therefore lies modestly above the sample mean in Agreeableness, Extraversion, Emotional Stability, and Openness, with Conscientiousness approximately average. This is a local density description, not a personality type.

The two finite bumps are numerically tiny:

- larger finite bump: basin size 18 (0.210%), prominence 0.120 log-density units;
- smaller finite bump: basin size 1 (0.012%), prominence 0.041 log-density units.

Neither qualifies for interpretation under the frozen 0.5% support rule.

## Interpretation

### Observed

At Big Five resolution, the terrain is not only connected. Under the frozen density-tree procedure it also lacks multiple material local density maxima. The few additional local maxima are tiny neighborhood irregularities rather than population-scale hills.

### Interpretation

The five broad Big Five dimensions appear to compress the respondent distribution into a single broad, internally uneven occupancy body. The earlier excess local-density heterogeneity is real relative to the Gaussian-copula null, but that heterogeneity is not organized into multiple material Big Five basins under this procedure.

### Consequence for the corridor question

A meaningful maximum-support corridor requires at least two retained peaks. Because the frozen analysis finds only one material peak, there is no legitimate Big Five peak-to-peak saddle or corridor to characterize. Creating one by selecting arbitrary endpoints after seeing the data would answer a different, post hoc question and is not done here.

### Next resolution

The natural next test is the ten BFAS aspects. This preserves an established psychological representation while allowing broad Big Five domains to separate into two aspects each. The same peak/saddle/corridor machinery can then test whether the single Big Five mound resolves into reproducible substructure at higher psychological resolution.
