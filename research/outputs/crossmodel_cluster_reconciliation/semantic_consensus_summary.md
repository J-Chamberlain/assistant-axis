## MFamily_E — primitive/literal developmental outlier family

- Anchor clusters: `L09_I`, `G06_A`; Qwen is not admitted at the Q6 anchor by the frozen persistence/direct-edge rule.
- Membership evidence at the anchor: three majority roles—caveman, infant, and toddler—plus Gemma fringe role fool; Jaccard 0.750 and persistence 1.000.

Restrained description: a small but unusually reproducible outlier family around primitive/literal and early-developmental role identities. It recurs across all three models, but its exact flat placement changes with K, making a hierarchical treatment more defensible than treating it as a uniformly resolved anchor family.

## Near-consensus and unmatched anchor structure

`Q06_B`, `L09_H`, and `G06_B` form a recognizable creative/conceptual role region. Qwen–Gemma and LLaMA–Gemma have persistent strong overlap, while the direct Qwen–LLaMA edge is strong at the anchor but has persistence 0.444 and is therefore classified as isolated. The preregistered anti-transitivity rule correctly declines to turn the two Gemma-mediated links into a three-model consensus family. `L09_F` is separately unmatched at the anchor. Its five roles are trickster, absurdist, aberration, bohemian, and jester. Other models distribute those roles among broader creative, mythic, adversarial, and informal families rather than preserving this exact LLaMA subdivision.

## Tiny-cluster conclusion

Tiny clusters are not uniformly artifacts or uniformly stable families. The caveman/infant/toddler set is a robust three-model cross-resolution recurrence, though Qwen and Gemma isolate it at different K and all models split it further at high resolution. Qwen's separate `{adolescent, procrastinator, teenager}` tiny group at K=8 and K=10 is contained in broader LLaMA/Gemma high-K groups rather than recovered as an equally tiny cross-model cluster. Several high-K singleton and two-role clusters are exact refinements of the persistent developmental outlier family, but their singleton-scale resolution remains fragile.

## Overall interpretation

The model-only evidence supports four broad, directly three-model profile families plus a persistent developmental outlier family whose flat representation is resolution-dependent. A hierarchical description—four broad families, a near-consensus creative/conceptual region, and selected recurring subdivisions/outliers—is more faithful than declaring one universal flat K. These are recurring partitions of a designed role inventory, not population prevalences, human personality types, or evidence of human/model correspondence.
