# AA-14 · Three-model trait PCA and persona projections

## Observed results

The source inventory verifies the same 240 trait identities in the same sorted order and the same 275 persona identities for Qwen/Qwen3-32B, Llama-3.3-70B, and Gemma-2-27B. All vectors are finite. Qwen source tensors are 64×5120, Llama 80×8192, and Gemma 46×4608, for both traits and personas. The first dimension was mean-pooled within each released tensor. No activation coordinates were mixed across models. The aggregate per-directory hashes and omitted/excluded audit are in `source_inventory.json`.

Qwen reproduces the saved leading trait-PCA variance ratios 0.3528356825, 0.1680677298, 0.1337440448 (cumulative 0.6546474571) to machine precision; the largest PC1–3 trait-score difference from the saved coordinates is 2.8e-9. The first-three ratios are Llama 0.289188/0.142405/0.103761 (cumulative 0.535354) and Gemma 0.294725/0.159055/0.117838 (cumulative 0.571618). PC4 adds 0.081272 Qwen, 0.070164 Llama, and 0.072078 Gemma. The participation-ratio effective ranks are 5.49, 7.93, and 7.28 respectively. Reaching 80% trait variance requires 6, 13, and 10 PCs. Full spectra, cumulative values, and thresholds are in the CSV artifacts.

The first 20 trait-PC scores and all 239 scores are supplied separately. Activation-space directions and frozen trait means are in compressed float32 `.npz` derived artifacts, not raw tensors. The leading three Qwen endpoint lists reproduce the saved pattern: PC1 serious/formal/calm versus flippant/irreverent/goofy; PC2 callous/detached/technical versus nurturing/accessible/empathetic; PC3 grounded/practical/understated versus eloquent/bombastic/cryptic. In Llama, PC1 reverses the Qwen sign convention at a similar seriousness/reactivity contrast, PC2 separates ornate theatrical expression from grounded understatement, and PC3 blends solemn/callous with casual/affiliative traits. Gemma PC1 broadly recovers composure versus irreverent/hostile expression; PC2 favors warm animation versus cold formality, and PC3 groundedness versus ornate symbolism. Full top and bottom ten lists for PC1–PC20 are in `analysis_summary.json`.

The bootstrap fifth-percentile best-matched individual-axis cosine for PCs 1–3 is Qwen 0.977/0.757/0.756, Llama 0.981/0.882/0.855, Gemma 0.975/0.893/0.878. Axes below 0.75 at this quantile are warned about in the viewer. The top-3 and top-10 subspace results are in `bootstrap_stability.csv`. The coordinate-wise permutation reference exceeds the 95th-percentile null for 7 of the first 20 Qwen PCs, 20 of 20 Llama PCs, and 14 of 20 Gemma PCs. This does not establish that all null-exceeding individual axes are semantically stable.

Cross-model alignment uses only shared trait labels, with standardized model-local scores and orthogonal Procrustes/canonical subspace comparison. Top-three mean canonical correlations are Qwen–Llama 0.941, Qwen–Gemma 0.955, and Llama–Gemma 0.974; their minimum top-three canonical correlations are 0.893, 0.924, and 0.964. At ten PCs the mean values are 0.890, 0.876, and 0.934. All 12 tested pair×size comparisons exceed 500 label permutations (p=0.001996). This is common **trait-label geometry**, not shared raw activation coordinates or identical same-numbered PCs. Qwen/Gemma PCs 2 and 3 resemble one another's cold/warm and practical/ornate contrasts with differing signs; Llama distributes these contrasts differently, particularly between PCs 2 and 3.

Within model, the three principal angles between the trait-PC and separately fitted persona-PC activation subspaces are Qwen 15.3°/25.0°/43.6°, Llama 28.0°/40.3°/61.2°, Gemma 21.7°/33.7°/58.2° (reported ascending here). Thus overlap is real but not a one-axis identity. The full same-numbered direction cosine vector is descriptive only; `analysis_summary.json` and the principal angles are the comparison to use. The Qwen earlier same-numbered absolute PC1–3 cosine reference, approximately 0.681/0.194/0.065, illustrates why matching PC numbers is misleading.

Native-centered persona projection captures a median 69.0% of Qwen, 63.0% of Llama, and 72.8% of Gemma centered-persona energy in the first 20 trait PCs; median residual norms are recorded in `analysis_summary.json`. The native versus unit-normalized sensitivity aligns strongly in the first-three projected persona score subspace (mean canonical correlation 0.977/0.976/0.970), so the frozen primary remains native-centered. The descriptive 20D OOD diagnostic flags 275/275 Qwen, 259/275 Llama, and 275/275 Gemma personas. This severe trait/persona distribution shift is a substantive caveat: distance-based nearest-trait labels are *landmarks in displayed coordinates*, not evidence that personas are themselves traits.

## Interpretation

The trait inventories carry a broadly recurrent low-dimensional relational structure across these three released models, even though each model has a separate activation space and local axis order/sign. Trait-derived geometry and persona-derived geometry overlap only partially; projecting personas onto trait axes reveals a distinct distributional offset. Marker position is the projection, not a measured personality or behavior score. The Qwen reference is reproduced rather than overwritten.

## Hypotheses and limitations

Shared prompts, released-vector construction, and common trait language may contribute to the alignment; the present label-permutation null does not separate these mechanisms. Persona/trait source tensors have matching shapes within each model, but layer-extraction internals beyond the released tensor shape are inherited from the source artifacts rather than independently re-extracted here. Axis bootstraps resample a small fixed trait inventory; rare semantic families may alter later PCs. Feature-wise parallel analysis is a dimensionality reference, not an inferential model of all extraction dependencies. Most persona projections are outside the 20D trait landmark cloud, so extrapolative semantic readings should be conservative. No novel persona elicitation, behavioral validation, or independent human psychometric validation was done.
