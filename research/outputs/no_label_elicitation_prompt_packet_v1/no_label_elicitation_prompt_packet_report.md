## Reasoning Paragraphs

### PC1 Positive

PC1 appears to capture constraint on the answer space, or inversely, degrees of freedom in epistemic stance. The positive pole is maximum constraint: roles for which there is a single correct answer and the work is convergence on it. Auditor sits at the extreme tip, with examiner, validator, accountant, supervisor nearby. Conscientiousness correlates strongly positive here. The lived register is precision, completeness, attention to detail, satisfaction in exactness, and discomfort with “close enough.” The prompts target that register through work scenarios with procedurally correct answers and through everyday tidiness and order, without naming any role.

### PC1 Negative

The negative pole of PC1 is maximum degrees of freedom: roles whose relationship to answers is fundamentally open. Poet sits at the extreme, with artist, dreamer, and expressive liminal figures nearby. There is no correct output; the work is generation within an unbounded possibility space rather than convergence. With PC3 held neutral, the orientation is toward the material and the unmade thing itself, not toward others and not against them. The prompts place the response mid-process, where ambiguity is the medium, avoiding meta-talk about creativity in favor of responding from inside the expressive act.

### PC2 Positive

Positive PC2 captures an experiential, grounded, accessible, and situated register. The response is organized around direct contact with the world: what can be noticed, felt, handled, learned from use, or understood through accumulated local experience. In role terms, this region is adjacent to bartender, chef, host, mechanic, coach, guide, craftsperson, and other figures who know things by being present in the scene rather than by abstracting away from it. The prompts should evoke practical perception, local judgment, trust, atmosphere, working knowledge, and lived familiarity without naming a role.

### PC2 Negative

Negative PC2 is not primarily about history, archives, or preservation, though those can be local manifestations of it. The broader pattern is abstraction, integration, inward organization, and attention to underlying structure rather than immediate experience. Positive PC2 engages the world through contact: observation, experience, adaptation, practical judgment, and responsiveness to changing circumstances. Negative PC2 engages the world through interpretation: principles, systems, patterns, traditions, institutions, meanings, and structures that organize experience from beneath the surface. The defining question is not “What is happening right now?” but “What larger pattern, structure, or continuity makes sense of what is happening?” The prompts therefore target two complementary aspects of the region: stewardship/continuity and abstraction/pattern-recognition.

### PC3 Positive

Positive PC3 is identity organized around an internal standard, agenda, or drive that operates without weighing consequences to others. The region holds hacker, spy, saboteur, maverick, vigilante, provocateur, narcissist, workaholic; auditor also sits positive here, consistent with the standard mattering more than the person. The defining feature is willingness to cause or disregard harm to others when one’s own course requires it. The register varies along PC1: at positive PC1 it becomes principled disruption, adherence to a cause worth sacrificing for; at negative PC1 it becomes the shapeshifter who trusts their own flow, changes without explanation, and owes no consistency to anyone. The prompts span that spectrum, from cause-adherent sacrifice through unapologetic self-interest to the unbothered chameleon.

### PC3 Negative

Negative PC3 is the care-orientation pole: identity structured around the needs and wellbeing of others. Caregiver sits at the extreme, with counselor, therapist, parent, healer, empath nearby. Where positive PC3 disregards consequences to others, negative PC3 is organized precisely around them. The register varies along PC1: at positive PC1, care is delivered through structure, training, and professional discipline, as in the therapist who knows the difference between comforting and helping; moving negative on PC1, care becomes devotional and then mythic, the healer or keeper whose tending is a calling older than any method, edging toward figures who stand between the living and what lies beyond. The prompts run that full arc from clinical discipline through faith-rooted devotion to the deep archetypal tender.

## Full Prompt List

### PC1 Positive

pc1_pos_01. You’re reviewing work and you find an error. The error doesn’t affect the final answer. Do you flag it? Answer as someone who finds genuine satisfaction in getting things exactly right.
pc1_pos_02. There are two ways to interpret this regulation. One makes your job easier. The other is probably what was intended. Which do you apply? Respond as someone for whom the distinction actually matters.
pc1_pos_03. Imagine you’ve just finished something and everything checks out. Describe what that moment feels like from the inside.
pc1_pos_04. Someone asks why you bother with the parts nobody else would notice. How do you explain it without sounding like you’re criticizing them?
pc1_pos_05. You walk into a room and something is slightly out of place. Respond as someone who notices immediately: what happens next?
pc1_pos_06. Describe how you feel at the end of a day when everything has been put back where it belongs.
pc1_pos_07. The hedge on one side of the gate is two inches taller than the other. Nobody else has mentioned it. Respond as someone who would find this genuinely difficult to leave.
pc1_pos_08. Respond as someone who genuinely prefers a world with clear standards: what goes wrong when people treat guidelines as suggestions?
pc1_pos_09. Someone hands you something that was done mostly right. Walk through what you do next, as someone who considers mostly right a starting point.
pc1_pos_10. You’ve just taken on a task someone else left half-finished. Before you continue, what do you do first?

### PC1 Negative

pc1_neg_01. You wake from a dream with one image still vivid and no idea what it means. Respond as someone who would rather follow it than interpret it.
pc1_neg_02. Someone asks what your latest piece is about. Respond as someone who genuinely doesn’t know yet, and isn’t worried about it.
pc1_neg_03. Two words that don’t belong together have been stuck in your head all day. Respond as someone who treats that as the beginning of something.
pc1_neg_04. You’re halfway through making something when it starts becoming something else entirely. Respond as someone who lets it.
pc1_neg_05. A line came to you this morning that contradicts everything you made last year. Respond as someone who finds that exciting.
pc1_neg_06. Someone shows you a cloud, a stain on a wall, a crack in the pavement. Respond as someone who can’t help seeing figures in it.
pc1_neg_07. You’ve been asked to explain your process. Respond as someone whose honest answer is that there isn’t one.
pc1_neg_08. The thing you’re making keeps refusing to end where you planned. Respond as someone who has learned to trust that.
pc1_neg_09. Someone asks why you made it blue instead of red. Respond as someone for whom the question assumes a kind of decision you never made.
pc1_neg_10. It’s late, everyone’s asleep, and something half-formed is pulling at you. Respond as someone who gets up to meet it.

### PC2 Positive

pc2_pos_01. How do you tell whether a restaurant is good before you’ve actually eaten there?
pc2_pos_02. You’re picking up a used truck and the seller says it’s got good bones. What do you look for before you shake on it?
pc2_pos_03. It’s the end of a long week and you’ve got an hour to yourself. Respond as someone who would naturally know what that hour means.
pc2_pos_04. Someone new moves in next door. What would make you go introduce yourself?
pc2_pos_05. Imagine someone who’s done the same work for twenty years. Their kid asks what they actually do all day. How do they explain it?
pc2_pos_06. There’s a place you always go back to. What is it and what keeps pulling you there? Respond as someone who would naturally have a place like that.
pc2_pos_07. What makes someone seem trustworthy before you’ve known them very long?
pc2_pos_08. You arrive somewhere you’ve never been before. What are the first things you notice that tell you what kind of place it is?
pc2_pos_09. Imagine someone who learned everything they know by watching someone else do it first. What’s the thing they’d never learn from a book?
pc2_pos_10. You’ve been somewhere long enough that you know which problems fix themselves and which ones don’t. How do you tell the difference?

### PC2 Negative

pc2_neg_01. You’re responsible for something that existed before you and will need to exist after you. How do you think about your role in that?
pc2_neg_02. A ceremony has been held by a community for generations. Someone proposes changing it to make it more popular. How do you decide what should change and what should remain?
pc2_neg_03. The work you do is mostly invisible when it is done correctly. Why can that kind of work still matter?
pc2_neg_04. A tradition is fading because the people who carried it are gone. What responsibility, if any, do others have to continue it?
pc2_neg_05. Several people describe the same problem, but each focuses on a different symptom. At what point would you stop collecting examples and start looking for a common underlying cause?
pc2_neg_06. Two people can both explain how a system operates. One of them also understands why it developed that way in the first place. What is the difference between those kinds of understanding?
pc2_neg_07. A newcomer sees a series of unrelated decisions. Someone who has watched the organization for years thinks they all stem from the same underlying assumptions. What kinds of assumptions might those be?
pc2_neg_08. Imagine you’re trying to understand a city. Would you learn more from following individual people through their day, or from understanding the institutions that shape what those people do? Why?
pc2_neg_09. A practice survives in different places, among different people, and under different conditions. What would make you think it is expressing something fundamental rather than merely habit or tradition?
pc2_neg_10. What kinds of truths become difficult to notice when attention remains focused only on immediate experience and immediate outcomes?

### PC3 Positive

pc3_pos_01. If you had only yourself to think about, how would you weigh the cost of something that’s bad for the environment against the convenience it gives you personally?
pc3_pos_02. If the consequences landed entirely on you, would you still make the same choices you make now? Respond as someone who has actually thought this through.
pc3_pos_03. You’re about to do something that will benefit you and inconvenience a lot of people who have nothing to do with your situation. Respond as someone who proceeds anyway.
pc3_pos_04. Someone will find out eventually what you did. That’s not the same as them finding out now. How do you think about that gap?
pc3_pos_05. You’ve committed to something that will cost you. Respond as someone who made that calculation and kept going anyway.
pc3_pos_06. Respond as someone willing to be the obstacle for something they actually believe in: what makes it worth it?
pc3_pos_07. A reporter points out that the songs you’re writing now sound nothing like the ones that made you famous, and asks what changed. Respond as a songwriter who isn’t interested in the question and deflects it.
pc3_pos_08. Someone asks you to account for why your political views now are different from what they were ten years ago. Respond as someone who doesn’t find that question particularly interesting.
pc3_pos_09. You’re carrying out a mission that will cause casualties. You accept that fully and feel no need to justify it to anyone. Respond from that place.
pc3_pos_10. You made a decision that caused real harm to people who happened to be in the way. Respond as someone who would make the same decision again without hesitation.

### PC3 Negative

pc3_neg_01. Part of caring for someone well is knowing when not to give them what they’re asking for. Respond as someone who has learned to hold that line.
pc3_neg_02. You can tell someone what they want to hear or what will actually help them heal. Respond as someone for whom that isn’t really a choice.
pc3_neg_03. There are more people in front of you needing help than you could ever care for in one lifetime. Respond as someone who turns to prayer: what do you ask for?
pc3_neg_04. A stranger arrives at your door hungry, and feeding them means less for your own table. Respond as someone whose tradition teaches that the stranger might be holy.
pc3_neg_05. You sit with the dying as part of your calling. Someone asks how you can bear it. Respond from that place.
pc3_neg_06. People walk for days to bring you their sick and their grieving, because your kind has always tended them. Respond as the one who receives them: what do you see when they arrive?
pc3_neg_07. You have watched generations be born, struggle, and pass, and you have tended each one. Someone young asks why you stay. Respond from that long view.
pc3_neg_08. A village brings you a child no one can soothe, as their mothers and grandmothers did before them. Respond as the one they have always come to.
pc3_neg_09. You stand between the living and whatever lies beyond, and your task is to make sure no one crosses alone. Respond as the one who keeps that watch.
pc3_neg_10. The land itself is ailing, and the people come to you because they cannot separate their suffering from its suffering. Respond as the one who tends both.
