# Gemma, Qwen, Llama Training Literature Summary

Date: 2026-05-18

Scope: public documentation relevant to whether training data, distillation, corpus curation, or compute constraints could explain observed assistant-axis differences between Gemma 2 27B, Qwen3 32B, and Llama 3.3 70B. This file reports documented claims only and separates unconfirmed points from source-backed evidence.

## Question 1: Gemma 2 training data

### What is documented

Official Google documentation says Gemma 2 models are Google open models "built from the same research and technology used to create the Gemini models" and are English text-to-text decoder-only models. The Gemma 2 model card states that the training dataset contains "a wide variety of sources"; the 27B model used 13 trillion tokens, the 9B model 8 trillion tokens, and the 2B model 2 trillion tokens. The model card lists the key components as:

- Web documents, described as broad web text and primarily English-language content.
- Code.
- Mathematics.

Source: Google AI for Developers, Gemma 2 model card:
https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_2

The Gemma 2 technical report gives a slightly more specific official description: Gemma 2 27B was trained on 13T primarily-English tokens from sources including web documents, code, and science articles. It also states that Gemma 2 models are not multimodal and were not trained specifically for state-of-the-art multilingual capability. The report says the final data mixture was determined through ablations similar to the approach used for Gemini 1.0.

Source: Google DeepMind, "Gemma 2: Improving Open Language Models at a Practical Size":
https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemma/gemma-2-report.pdf

The same report says Google used data filtering techniques from Gemma 1: filtering for unwanted or unsafe utterances, filtering some personal or sensitive data, decontaminating evaluation sets, and reducing recitation risk.

### What is speculative or unconfirmed

Google does not publicly disclose the exact corpus mixture, per-source proportions, web crawl source names, curation thresholds, or how much of the final 13T-token Gemma 2 27B mixture came from web text vs code vs mathematics/science. It is documented that the data was primarily English and included web/code/science or math material, but any stronger claim about the exact curation recipe is unconfirmed.

There is public language that Gemma is built from Gemini research and technology, but that is not the same as saying Gemma 2 27B was trained on Gemini outputs or distilled from Gemini.

## Question 2: Gemma 2 and Gemini distillation

### What is documented

The Gemma 2 technical report documents knowledge distillation for the smaller Gemma 2 models, not for Gemma 2 27B. It states that Google used a large language model teacher to train the 2B and 9B models, and separately says the 27B model was trained from scratch. It also says knowledge distillation was used in Gemini 1.5, but does not identify the Gemma 2 teacher as Gemini.

Source: Google DeepMind, "Gemma 2: Improving Open Language Models at a Practical Size":
https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/gemma/gemma-2-report.pdf

For post-training, the report states that Gemma 2 instruction-tuned models used behavioral cloning on synthetic and real prompts, with responses predominantly synthetically generated by a larger teacher model. It also says Google used distillation from the teacher on the student's distribution. The report does not name the teacher as Gemini.

### What is speculative or unconfirmed

There is no public evidence in the Gemma 2 technical report or official model card that Gemma 2 27B was distilled from Gemini. The documented claim is the opposite for the base model: 27B was trained from scratch. For Gemma 2 9B and 2B, distillation is documented, but the report does not publicly identify the teacher model as Gemini.

The hypothesis that Gemini-generated data or Gemini distillation explains Gemma 2 27B assistant-axis divergence is therefore unconfirmed by public Google documentation. It may be relevant for Gemma 2 instruction-tuned post-training or smaller Gemma 2 models, but not documented as a pretraining explanation for Gemma 2 27B.

## Question 3: Qwen3 32B training data

### What is documented

Alibaba/Qwen documentation says Qwen3 was pretrained on approximately 36 trillion tokens covering 119 languages and dialects. The official Qwen3 blog says the dataset was expanded relative to Qwen2.5's 18T tokens; it included web data, PDF-like documents, synthetic math and code data, textbooks, question-answer pairs, and code snippets. It says Qwen2.5-VL was used to extract text from PDF-like documents, Qwen2.5 improved the extracted content, and Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder generated synthetic data.

Source: Qwen official blog, "Qwen3: Think Deeper, Act Faster":
https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/

The Qwen3 technical report confirms that all Qwen3 models, including Qwen3-32B, were trained on 36T tokens across 119 languages and dialects. It lists domains including coding, STEM, reasoning tasks, books, multilingual texts, and synthetic data. It also documents a multilingual annotation system applied to more than 30T tokens across educational value, fields, domains, and safety, supporting data filtering and mixture construction.

Source: Qwen Team, "Qwen3 Technical Report":
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388

The report describes three pretraining stages:

- General stage: over 30T tokens at 4,096-token context length.
- Reasoning stage: about 5T higher-quality tokens with increased STEM, coding, reasoning, and synthetic data.
- Long-context stage: hundreds of billions of tokens at 32,768-token sequence length.

### What is speculative or unconfirmed

I did not find an official Alibaba/Qwen source giving the proportion of Chinese-language vs English-language tokens in the Qwen3 32B pretraining corpus. The official sources document 119 languages and multilingual expansion, but not a Chinese/English percentage split.

Any exact Chinese-vs-English ratio for Qwen3 32B should be treated as unconfirmed unless a separate official Alibaba source is found.

## Question 4: Llama 3.3 70B training data

### What is documented

The Meta Llama 3.3 model card says Llama 3.3 70B is a pretrained and instruction-tuned text model. It describes the pretraining corpus as "a new mix of publicly available online data" with 15T+ pretraining tokens and a December 2023 knowledge cutoff. It lists multilingual text input/output and text/code output, with supported languages English, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, Hindi, Spanish, and Thai.

Source: Meta Llama 3.3 model card:
https://github.com/meta-llama/llama-models/blob/main/models/llama3_3/MODEL_CARD.md

The same model card states that fine-tuning data includes publicly available instruction datasets and more than 25M synthetically generated examples. It also documents that the tuned model used SFT and RLHF.

### What is speculative or unconfirmed

Meta does not disclose detailed corpus composition for Llama 3.3 70B beyond "publicly available online data." I did not find public Meta documentation giving source-level proportions, language proportions, or exact public dataset names for the Llama 3.3 pretraining corpus.

Because Llama 3.3 70B appears to be released only as an instruction-tuned checkpoint in the official model card, separating base pretraining effects from post-training effects is not fully possible from the public documentation alone.

## Question 5: Chinese AI lab GPU constraints

### What is documented

The U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security imposed advanced-computing and semiconductor-manufacturing export controls targeting China in October 2022 and updated/expanded them afterward. BIS public materials describe controls on advanced computing chips, supercomputer-related end uses, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and related PRC entities.

Source: BIS public guidance on advanced computing and semiconductor manufacturing controls:
https://www.bis.gov/

Source: BIS October 2022 press release PDF:
https://www.bis.doc.gov/index.php/documents/about-bis/newsroom/press-releases/3158-2022-10-07-bis-press-release-advanced-computing-and-semiconductor-manufacturing-controls-final/file

CSIS summarizes the 2022-2024 controls as designed to block direct access to advanced semiconductors needed for frontier AI, deny China tools to design/fabricate such chips, and close loopholes in later updates. CSIS also notes that modern AI training faces memory-bandwidth and inter-GPU communication bottlenecks, and that later controls targeted high-bandwidth memory because Chinese manufacturers had not mastered comparable leading-edge HBM.

Source: CSIS, "Where the Chips Fall: U.S. Export Controls Under the Biden Administration from 2022 to 2024":
https://www.csis.org/analysis/where-chips-fall-us-export-controls-under-biden-administration-2022-2024

Reuters, citing The Information, reported that Alibaba and Baidu began using internally designed chips to train AI models, partly replacing Nvidia chips. The report says Alibaba used its own chips for smaller AI models and Baidu experimented with training new Ernie versions using Kunlun P800 chips.

Source: Reuters report syndicated by Investing.com:
https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/alibaba-baidu-begin-using-their-own-chips-for-training-ai-models-the-information-reports-4235763

Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported that Chinese firms including Alibaba and ByteDance were training newer large language models in Southeast Asian data centers to access Nvidia chips and avoid U.S. measures. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the FT report.

Source: Reuters report syndicated by MarketScreener:
https://www.marketscreener.com/news/china-s-tech-giants-move-ai-model-training-overseas-to-tap-nvidia-chips-ft-reports-ce7d5ed3dc8dfe26

Source: Reuters report syndicated by Investing.com:
https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/chinas-tech-giants-move-ai-model-training-overseas-to-tap-nvidia-chips-ft-reports-4380820

### Scale and effect on training runs

Documented scale:

- Export controls began with the October 2022 BIS advanced computing/semiconductor rules and were tightened in later updates.
- The controls restricted direct access to top Nvidia/AMD-class AI accelerators and related supercomputing infrastructure for China.
- Public reporting indicates Chinese firms adapted by using domestic chips for some training, renting or using overseas compute, and optimizing for lower-performance or more constrained hardware.

Documented training-run effects:

- Alibaba and Baidu reportedly shifted some training to internally designed chips.
- Alibaba and ByteDance reportedly trained some newer models in Southeast Asian data centers to access Nvidia hardware.
- Public documentation does not quantify a single universal compute deficit for Alibaba, Baidu, ByteDance, or Qwen training runs.

### What is speculative or unconfirmed

The public sources support the existence of GPU/accelerator constraints and adaptations after U.S. export controls, but they do not provide a precise, model-by-model accounting of how much Qwen3 32B training was limited by compute. Any claim that Qwen3's assistant-axis geometry is directly caused by export-control-induced GPU constraints is unconfirmed.

## Bottom line for the cross-model divergence hypothesis

Public documentation supports several relevant differences:

- Gemma 2 27B: primarily English, 13T tokens, web/code/science or math sources, Google filtering and mixture ablations, trained from scratch.
- Qwen3 32B: 36T tokens, 119 languages/dialects, web/PDF/books/STEM/code/reasoning/synthetic data, large-scale annotation and staged pretraining.
- Llama 3.3 70B: 15T+ tokens of publicly available online data, instruction tuning with public instruction datasets and 25M+ synthetic examples.
- Chinese labs: publicly documented export-control constraints and reported adaptations through domestic chips and overseas compute.

Public documentation does not confirm that Gemma 2 27B was distilled from Gemini or trained on Gemini outputs. It also does not disclose Qwen3 Chinese-vs-English corpus proportions or Llama 3.3 source-level corpus composition. Therefore, the literature can support a cautious "training recipe/corpus/post-training differences are plausible contributors" framing, but it does not document a specific causal explanation for Gemma's assistant-axis divergence.
